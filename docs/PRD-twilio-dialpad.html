1. Executive summary

Twilio Dialpad is a Chrome extension that turns your own Twilio account into a full calling app (a "softphone") that lives in Chrome's side panel. You make and take phone calls right in the browser. Unlike the big calling platforms, you bring your own Twilio account, so you pay Twilio's raw per-minute rate instead of a marked-up per-seat subscription.

Its standout feature: every call can be transcribed, and those transcripts connect to AI assistants (like Claude) through a personal link. You can literally ask an AI "how did my calls go today?" and get real answers — coaching, summaries, objection patterns — with zero manual work.

It is built for solo salespeople and cold-callers who already use Twilio (or are willing to set it up) and want a fast, cheap dialer plus AI call intelligence, without paying for an enterprise platform.

What it is

  • Self-hosted on your own Twilio account

2. The problem & why now

The problem

  • Calling platforms are expensive per seat. Aircall, JustCall, Dialpad (the company), Kixie and similar charge $30–$80+ per user per month, often with annual contracts. For a solo rep or a tiny team, that is a lot for "a dialer."
  • People who already have Twilio have no good front-end. Twilio gives you the raw calling infrastructure cheaply, but no usable everyday phone UI. Building one yourself is a project.
  • Call intelligence is locked behind enterprise tools. Transcription, call summaries, and coaching usually live in Gong/Chorus-class products priced for teams, not individuals.

Why now

  • AI assistants (ChatGPT, Claude) are mainstream — a solo rep will happily paste a link and ask an AI about their calls.
  • Cheap, accurate speech-to-text (Deepgram and similar) makes per-call transcription affordable for one person.
  • The "bring your own infrastructure + thin software layer" model is increasingly accepted by technical buyers who want to avoid per-seat SaaS markups.

5. What the product does (feature set)

Core calling

  • Side-panel dialpad — call any phone number or another Twilio client
  • Inbound calls — ring, accept/reject, desktop notifications
  • In-call controls — mute, keypad tones (DTMF), hang up
  • Call history — recent calls with number, direction, duration
  • Auto-reconnect & token refresh — stays connected reliably

Productivity

  • Auto-dialer — load a list of numbers and power-dial through a queue
  • One-click setup wizard — automatically creates the needed Twilio pieces (API key, call routing, and the small server-side functions) so most users never touch the Twilio console manually
  • Paste-to-dial — paste a number straight into the dialpad

AI & intelligence (the differentiator)

  • Live transcription — each call transcribed in real time (optional, uses a transcription key)
  • Claude / AI connector — a personal link you paste into Claude so an AI can read your call history and transcripts and answer questions, summarize, and surface patterns
  • Optional transcript export to a local folder

CRM

  • HubSpot lookup — incoming/known numbers matched to a HubSpot contact so you see who's calling

6. How it works (plain-English journey)

  1. Install from the Chrome Web Store.
  2. Connect Twilio — paste your Twilio credentials once; the wizard auto-configures everything behind the scenes.
  3. Start a 7-day free trial — no card required.
  4. Make calls from the side panel; calls route through your own Twilio account.
  5. (Optional) turn on transcription and connect Claude with a personal link.
  6. Subscribe ($9/mo) to keep cloud sync + AI features after the trial; local calling continues regardless.
The "aha" moment is the first successful call — and, more powerfully, seeing a transcript appear and asking an AI about it. Getting users to that moment fast is the single biggest growth lever (see §9).

Category: Browser-based sales dialer / softphone with built-in AI call intelligence.

Position: The cheapest serious dialer for solo callers who want AI notes — by bringing your own Twilio and paying only a thin software fee.

One-line positioning: "Aircall-style calling + Gong-style AI notes, for one person, at 1/5th the price — on top of your own Twilio."

Naming risk (important for marketing/SEO): "Dialpad" is also a large, well-funded calling company. 9. Business model & pricing

  • Free 7-day trial — no card required. Local calling works.
  • $9 / month after trial — unlocks cloud sync + the AI/Claude connector.
  • Payment processor: Dodo Payments (merchant-of-record; handles tax/VAT). Net ≈ $8.05 per $9 after fees (~10.5%).
  • Cost to deliver: near-zero infra (users pay their own Twilio + transcription). Backend is lightweight.
Pricing posture: at this stage, keep $9 and focus on volume + activation, not price changes. With ~1 paying user there is no statistical basis to re-price. Consider an annual plan (e.g. ~$90/yr) for upfront cash & lower churn once there are more payers.

Two confirmed problems

  1. The setup wall. Needing Twilio + pasting credentials stops most installs before the first call. No instant "it works" moment after install.
  2. The AI feature is under-adopted. The transcription/AI layer — the actual differentiator and the reason to pay — requires an extra optional step most users skip, so they never feel the magic that justifies $9/mo.

Acquisition (more installs)

  • Communities where solo cold-callers & Twilio users gather (Reddit r/sales, r/twilio, indie-hacker and SDR communities) — the launch Reddit post already proved this channel works.

Activation (installs → first call)

  • Make the first call effortless and guaranteed (guided first call ending in a transcript users can see).
  • Lower or soften the Twilio setup wall; reduce credential-paste anxiety.
  • Surface the AI value early so people reach the reason-to-pay sooner.

Monetization (activate → paid)

  • Tie the paywall to the moment of clearest value (after they see an AI call summary).
  • Add an annual plan for cash & retention.
  • Talk to the heavy users (89/53/41 calls) — learn what they'd pay more for.
